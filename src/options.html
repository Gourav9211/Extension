Settings

Engine

Reads the opponent's chess.com rating and adjusts automatically: vs lower-rated players it deviates to 2nd/3rd-best moves more often and replies a touch faster; vs higher-rated ones it plays the engine's best more often and matches their pace more closely.

Clicks the suggested move on the board automatically. Engine assistance and automation violate chess.com fair-play rules and can get your account banned - intended for practice against the computer.

Auto-play timing

Humanised think times for auto-played moves. "Match opponent" replies just faster than their measured pace. "Balance with both players" also replies faster than the opponent, using their pace as the reference. "Random window" uses fixed min/max values. The "Max move time" cap applies to all modes. Occasionally a quiet position gets an alternative ("normal") move instead of the engine's best.

Alternative moves are only played in quiet positions: no mate on the board, evaluation within the limit above, no tablebase verdict. Mates and critical positions always get the engine's best move.

Maia (human-like moves)

Maia 3 is a chess transformer trained on real human games. Instead of "best move, or the 2nd best every so often", it plays the move a player of the chosen strength would actually pick. Runs entirely on-device - the network is bundled with the extension and no position ever leaves your computer.

The rating below is fed to the network rather than choosing a separate file, so either model covers every rating. The 3M model is an ablation trained without the positional-bias components; it plays plausibly but noticeably worse.

0 means "assume the opponent is the same strength as the player", which is usually what you want when Maia is playing both sides. Set it to model a specific opponent.
